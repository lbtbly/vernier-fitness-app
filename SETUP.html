Tailscale HTTPS certificates Unblocks the most

Until this toggle is on there is no HTTPS origin. With no HTTPS origin there are no passkeys, no Home Screen install, no push notifications and no offline storage on the iPhone. It is one switch and it takes about fifteen seconds.

  1. Open login.tailscale.com/admin/dns.
  2. Scroll to HTTPS Certificates.
  3. Click Enable HTTPS and confirm. MagicDNS is already on, so nothing else is needed.
02

Bring the iPhone back onto the tailnet

Tailscale shows iphone181 as last seen 23 days ago. The phone has to be on the tailnet to open the app at all, and installing it to the Home Screen is what makes push and offline storage legal on iOS.

  1. Unlock the iPhone, open the Tailscale app, connect.
  2. Check the iOS version: Settings, General, About. Push needs 16.4 or newer. The keep-the-screen-awake feature during a workout needs 18.4 or newer.
  3. Confirm iCloud Keychain and two-factor authentication are on, in Settings under your name. Passkeys will not sync to the phone without both.

Just the number. It decides whether Wake Lock during a session is available or has to degrade gracefully.

Withings developer application

This is what lets the scale sync by itself. Without it the app runs on its demo dataset, which is clearly labelled as demo and never mixed with real readings.

  1. Sign in at developer.withings.com/dashboard with your Withings account.
  2. Create an application. Choose the Public API integration type.
  3. Register this exact callback URL:
https://lamberts-mac-mini.tailda76d6.ts.net/api/integrations/withings/callback
  1. Copy the Client ID and the Client Secret into the fields below. The secret is shown once. If you lose it, rotate it in the dashboard and paste the new one.
05

Google Calendar OAuth client

Read your busy periods to place sessions, and write only into a dedicated calendar the app creates. It never writes to your primary calendar. Step 6 is the one that silently breaks things later, so do not skip it.

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and create a project, any name.
  2. Enable the Google Calendar API in APIs and Services, Library.
  3. Open Google Auth Platform, then Branding, and fill the minimum: app name, your email, developer contact.
  4. Under Audience, set the user type to External.
  5. Under Clients, create a client of type Web application, and add this authorized redirect URI:
http://localhost:3000/api/integrations/google/callback
Localhost, not the ts.net name, and that is deliberate. Google exempts localhost from its HTTPS rule, while a ts.net redirect collides with the Authorized domains requirement in a way Google does not document for unverified apps. You will connect Google once, from a browser on this Mac. Everything after that works from the phone.
  1. Back under Audience, click Publish app so the status reads In production. Leave it in Testing and Google expires your refresh token seven days after you connect, and the calendar silently stops working. Unverified in production is fine for one user: you will see a warning screen once and click through it.
  2. Copy the client ID and client secret below. The secret is shown once; if lost, use Add Secret on the client page.

Anthropic API key

Used for the meal photo estimate, menu generation and lab explanations, each behind its own consent toggle you can switch off separately. The key stays server-side and the app enforces a monthly spend ceiling you set.

  1. Go to console.anthropic.com, API keys, Create key.
  2. Make sure the account has credit. A key with no credit returns 429 and the app will report it as such rather than failing quietly.
  3. Paste it below and pick a monthly ceiling. Photo estimates cost roughly a cent each at current prices, so ten dollars is generous.

One cleanup that is my fault

While proving whether a third party can sign an installable .shortcut file, a research agent ran shortcuts sign --mode anyone twice on this Mac. That turns out to be the same operation as Copy iCloud Link, so it published two throwaway public shortcut records named Test under your Apple ID. They hold no personal data. That probe should have been read-only.

  1. Open the Shortcuts app on this Mac.
  2. Find the two shortcuts named Test.
  3. Right click each, choose Stop Sharing, then delete them.
