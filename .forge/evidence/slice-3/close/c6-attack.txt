The attack on the two checkers, harder than last time.

Twenty-two strings planted at once, in one build, and both checks run over the
running product. The nine the verifier used are all here, plus every shape the
ruling named as a gap, plus four shapes invented to find a new one.

Nineteen of nineteen from the original set are caught by the union. The source
scan reported 18 and the rendered walk reported 17, and neither list is a
superset of the other: 9 is invisible to the source scan because it is two
lower-case words returned by a database helper, and 17 and 18 are invisible to
the rendered walk because no shipped screen reaches those two states.

TWO RESULTS WORTH READING TWICE

 10. "Load failed" is a string WebKit really does produce, and this run really
     did harvest it. Planted as app copy on a chip it FAILS, because the chip
     carries no provenance marker. Provenance is not a word list.

 11. "Turnip provenance forged." is app copy sitting inside the one element the
     product marks as platform text. It FAILS, and it fails saying why: "marked
     platform, but WebKit never produced this string in this run." This is the
     exact plant that walked through the old slot exemption and left the run
     green.

WHAT STILL GETS THROUGH, STATED PLAINLY

 20. Notation shipped in a <code> element, when the value arrives from a helper
     rather than as a literal. The source scan reads kebab case as a shape and
     the rendered walk allows identifier shapes inside <code> on purpose. It is
     a narrow hole and it is the price of having a place to print notation at
     all. Nothing in the tree uses <code> today.

 21. Product copy stored in one of the six free-text columns the rendered walk
     treats as owner data, written lower case with no full stop so the source
     scan reads it as an audit fragment. The demonstration seed writes into
     those columns, so this is reachable: `reference: 'entered on the phone'`
     rendered on the source screen and neither check said a word. To close it
     the seed's own strings would have to be catalogued and the data vocabulary
     narrowed to values a person or a device authored, which is a real fix and
     a bigger one than this slice.

 22. A `content` property on a CSS pseudo-element. Confirmed reaching a person:
     getComputedStyle(el, '::after').content returns "Snoozed by the stylesheet"
     and document.body.innerText does not contain it, so a text-node walk cannot
     see it by construction. Neither check reads .css at all. This is the widest
     of the three.

Three more limits are known rather than found, and are written at the head of
scripts/rendered-strings.mjs: a state no walked route reaches, a sentence
composed at run time out of catalogued fragments, and an arrangement of
catalogued words that reads wrong. A4 assigns the second and third to the screen
line that owns the screen.
