# Ruling: the demonstration seed and the real Withings connection

2026-08-23, architect. Raised by slice 5's builder while building the importer.
One question, ruled once, across slices 4, 5 and 13.

The bar is unchanged. 166 lines, PASS at one hundred percent. No line is edited
by this ruling and no amendment is warranted. Section 5 says why, line by line.

---

## 1. What actually happens today

The builder's report contains one claim that the code does not support, and it
is the claim that makes the problem look worse than it is:

> the chart carries two points a day for every day the demonstration series
> covers

**It does not.** Every chart in this product draws exactly one point per
calendar day, always, by construction. The real problem is smaller in the
chart, larger in the archive, and different in kind from a duplicate. It is
this:

**The write layer resolves a day by rank. The read layer resolves a day by
clock. They disagree, and where they disagree the read layer can draw the
fabricated reading and hide the measured one.**

### The write path, which is correct

`storeMeasurement` at `src/db/measurements.ts:140` gathers a 72-hour window of
candidates at `:89` and hands them to `decideDedup` at
`src/lib/provenance.ts:260`.

For a real Withings weight arriving on a day the seed already owns, step 3 at
`:313` finds the seeded row, compares `PRECEDENCE` at `:237` (`withings` 4,
`demo` 0), takes `mine >= theirs`, and stores the real row with
`dedup_decision = 'kept_over_duplicate'` and a reason naming the demonstration
value it beat. That is right. The seeded row stays, because the table refuses
DELETE at `drizzle/0003_provenance.sql:96`.

So per overlapping day the table holds two rows: one real, one seeded, both
chain heads, both `correction_index` 0, each honestly stamped. `measurement.seed`
is generated by Postgres from `source` at `src/db/schema.ts:341`, so the seeded
row cannot claim to be real and the real one cannot claim to be seeded, and the
composite foreign key at `:356` refuses either mismatch.

### Chart

**One point per day, and it can be the wrong one.**

`readTrend` at `src/db/trends.ts:51` returns every row in the window with
corrections applied by `heads()` at `:38`, which collapses by `root_id` and by
nothing else. Two rows with different roots both survive. `readBodyScreen` at
`src/db/screens.ts:57` passes them to `dailySeries` at `src/lib/trend.ts:77`,
which keys a Map by day and keeps one reading per key. The tiebreak is at `:82`:

```
if (!held || reading.measuredAt.getTime() > held.measuredAt.getTime()) byDay.set(on, reading);
```

Latest instant wins. Rank is never consulted. `dotsOf` at
`src/lib/chart.ts:98` then draws one dot per `Day`, so two rows can never
produce two dots.

The seed writes weight at 06:52 local, fixed, at `src/db/demo.ts:590`. A
Withings reading lands at whatever time the owner stepped on the scale.

- He weighs at 07:14. The real reading wins the column. Correct by accident.
- He weighs at 06:30. **The fabricated reading wins the column, silently.** The
  chart, the 7-day average, the headline number, the week change, the pace and
  the goal delta are all computed off `days` in
  `src/lib/weight-summary.ts:51`, so all of them read the fabricated number.
  The dot links to `/value/<seeded id>`, so the one tap that is supposed to
  answer "where did this come from" answers "Demonstration" and the real
  reading is not on the screen at all.
- Both at the same second: `>` is strict, so the winner is whichever row the
  database returned first. Not deterministic.

The chip beside the headline is honest either way: `src/app/(shell)/page.tsx:282`
passes `summary.latest.origin` and `summary.latest.seed`, and `SourceChip` at
`src/components/SourceChip.tsx:50` draws the dotted demonstration edge. So the
failure is not that the app lies about the row it drew. It is that it drew the
wrong row and told the truth about it.

### Trend

Same defect, one layer up, because every trend figure is computed from the
resolved series and not from the rows. `rollingAverage` at
`src/lib/trend.ts:163`, `weightSummary` at `src/lib/weight-summary.ts:51`,
`findGaps` at `src/lib/trend.ts:99` all read `Day[]`. Feed them the fabricated
column and every sentence on the weight card is about a number nobody weighed.

One further trend effect, and it is the one slice 5 will trip over. `readFreshness`
at `src/db/trends.ts:173` groups `max(measured_at)` by **`source_record.source`**,
not by `measurement.source`. The seed writes a Withings-shaped source record
every morning at `src/db/demo.ts:592`, `source: 'withings'`, `kind: 'sync_batch'`.
So on this install Withings reads fresh every day it seeds, whether or not a
scale exists. F43's evidence, "name the stale Withings data by cause with the
adapter unreachable", cannot be produced on the owner's install as it stands.
It has to be produced where no Withings-shaped seed rows are being written, or
after this ruling lands.

### Archive

**This is where the two rows are actually visible, and it is where the honest
half stops being honest.**

Four surfaces resolve "which row stands", and each has its own copy of the rule:

| site | file | rule |
|---|---|---|
| write | `src/lib/provenance.ts:313` | rank, then reason |
| chart and trend | `src/lib/trend.ts:82` | latest instant |
| Today's "on file" card | `src/db/measurements.ts:343` | latest instant per metric |
| quick log's last waist | `src/db/measurements.ts:356` | latest instant |
| last reading of a metric | `src/db/trends.ts:125` | latest instant |

`readLatestPerMetric` feeds the "on file" card at `src/app/(shell)/page.tsx:602`.
On a collision day where the seed is later, that card shows the fabricated
weight with a Demonstration chip while a measured weight sits in the table
unshown. That is the archive answering the wrong question.

Two smaller archive facts, both permanent because the table refuses UPDATE:

- Every seeded row carries `dedup_decision = 'unique'` and the frozen sentence
  `strings.provenance.seeded` at `src/lib/strings.ts:791`: **"Seeded. Nothing
  else described this metric on this day."** The seed writes it directly at
  `src/db/demo.ts:761` and `:952` without ever calling `decideDedup`. The
  moment a real reading lands on that day the sentence is false, on the value
  sheet at `src/app/(shell)/value/[id]/page.tsx:122`, for ever.
- The visually hidden data table, which is what VoiceOver reads, prints
  `sourceLabel(day.reading.origin)` at `src/components/WeightChart.tsx:63` and
  renders it at `src/components/Chart.tsx:81`. `origin` is `source_record.source`,
  which is `withings` for a seeded weight. **A screen reader is told "Withings"
  for a fabricated reading, with no demonstration marker, while the sighted
  chip beside it is dotted.** `Reading.seed` is available at
  `src/lib/trend.ts:60` and unused there. Today that is a parity gap. The day
  real Withings rows sit beside seeded ones it becomes a table where the same
  word means two different things.

### Export

Nothing ships yet. There is no export route on main; slice 13 owns it under
F130. When it ships, a full export of `measurement` will contain both rows,
and that is correct, provided `source` and `seed` travel with every row. Two
rows for one morning, one marked as measured and one marked as fabricated, is
the truth about what this database holds. An export that collapsed them would
be the lie.

### One more collision, in slice 5's own demonstration mode

F36 requires that in demonstration mode every imported row carries source
`demo`. Those rows go through `storeMeasurement` and meet the seed's rows.
`decideDedup` step 3 at `src/lib/provenance.ts:313` only looks for a row whose
source **differs** (`row.source !== incoming.source`). Two `demo` rows on one
day with different values match nothing at steps 1, 2 or 3 and fall through to
`unique` at `:332`. Both are stored. Both claim to be the only reading of that
metric on that day.

For real Withings this is covered by A1's unique index on `(source, external_key)`.
For demonstration mode there is no shared external key, so it is not covered.

## 2. The recommendation

**One rule, in one sentence, applied at both layers: the demonstration fills
absence and yields to measurement.**

Concretely, two clauses of the same rule:

1. **The seed writes a metric only while that metric has no real reading.**
   One query per run, `select distinct metric from measurement where seed = false`,
   read once in `seedDemoData` and once in `continueDemoSeries`. Every metric in
   that set is skipped, in the anchor rows, in the six-week series and in the
   daily continuation. On an empty database the set is empty and nothing
   changes, so the scratch instance, the C6 floor and F25's own evidence are
   untouched.

2. **A day resolves to the measured reading when there is one.** One shared
   comparator, total and deterministic: a row with `seed = false` beats a row
   with `seed = true`; then the later `measured_at`; then the greater `id`.
   Used by `dailySeries`, `readLatestPerMetric`, `readLatestOfMetric` and
   `readLastReading`, so the five resolution sites become one rule with four
   callers.

Nothing is deleted. Nothing is updated. No row already in the table moves.

### Why this one

**Because the defect is not duplication, it is two dedup rules.** `decideDedup`
already ruled that the real row wins the day and wrote that ruling onto the
row: `kept_over_duplicate`, with the demonstration value named in the reason.
The read layer then ignores it and asks the clock instead. Every other option
on the table treats the symptom. This one makes the product answer the same
question the same way in every place it is asked, which is the only fix that
also covers the next source, the next metric and the next slice.

**Because it uses a fact Postgres computes and no writer can set.**
`measurement.seed` is `generated always as ("source" = 'demo')`. The comparator
rests on a column that cannot be forged, not on an id prefix and not on a
convention. That is the same reasoning slice 3 used when it made `seed` a
column, applied to the read path it never reached.

**Because it does not need the escape hatch and does not widen it.** No DELETE,
no TRUNCATE, no trigger disabled, no grant added. Which matters more than
usual right now: `a7bf989c` is the only real row in this database and there is
no backup in the tree until slice 13.

**Because it keeps the demonstration useful through the transition.** The
seeded past stays and keeps the 7-day average alive on the day the real scale
connects; only the future yields. The chart does not go blank and then refill.

**Because it is small enough to re-do.** Two files under a hundred lines of
change, both pure, both gradeable by the unit suite without a round trip.

### What it fixes as a consequence

- The seeded Withings-shaped source records stop the day the real weight
  starts arriving, so `readFreshness` becomes a fact about the real scale and
  F43's evidence becomes producible on the owner's own install.
- No new row is written carrying "Nothing else described this metric on this
  day" onto a day that will later hold a real reading. The false sentences that
  already exist are bounded, dated and permanent, which is what an append-only
  table means.
- The equal-timestamp nondeterminism at `src/lib/trend.ts:82` goes away.

### The four options refused, briefly

- **Turn `DEMO_MODE` off before connecting.** Refused, and it is the one the
  owner is most likely to reach for, so it is worth saying plainly: **it does
  not empty the chart and it does not remove one row.** No read path consults
  `DEMO_MODE`; `demoModeOn()` is read at `src/db/demo.ts:665` and `:828` and
  nowhere else. Turning it off freezes the series where it stands and leaves
  every seeded row on screen, so the install grows a trailing hole one day at a
  time and the 7-day average loses its window. That is precisely the failure
  `src/lib/demo-series.ts:72-90` was written to prevent. It is the worst option
  available and it looks like the safest.
- **The importer refuses a day the seed owns.** Refused. It inverts precedence:
  a fabricated number would beat a measured one on a product whose premise is
  provenance. There is no version of this product where that is the right
  answer.
- **Display-only, leave the seed writing.** Insufficient. The screens would be
  correct and the table would accrue one fabricated row per morning for ever,
  and Withings would keep reading fresh off rows no scale produced.
- **Seed-stop only, leave the display resolving by clock.** Insufficient. The
  six weeks already written still collide with the historical import, and on
  those days a 06:30 weigh-in still loses to the 06:52 fabrication, silently.

### The class, named once

Five places in this product answer "which row stands for this metric on this
day" and four of them answer it differently from the one that wrote the answer
down. The rule belongs in `src/lib/provenance.ts`, where it already lives, with
callers rather than copies. The builder's importer fix, "equal rank means the
committed row stays", is a sixth copy in the making: it is correct, and it
should land as a clause of `decideDedup` rather than beside it, because a
second copy of the rule is how the first two came to disagree.

That same edit closes the F36 hole: step 3's predicate at
`src/lib/provenance.ts:313` must stop requiring a different source, so two
`demo` rows on one day meet the rule instead of falling through to `unique`.

## 3. Who does it, in which slice, in what order

**Both clauses ship in slice 5.** Slice 5 is titled "Withings, demo and real"
and this is the seam between demo and real. It is one builder dispatch on top
of work already done.

Clause 2 touches `src/lib/trend.ts` and `src/db/measurements.ts`, which are
slice 4's and slice 3's files, under the ticked F30, F31, F32 and F20. That is
allowed and it is the established shape: slice 4 repaired C5, F23 and F24
without moving them into its `Closes:` list, and `.forge/PROCESS.md` section 1
is explicit that a ticked line is not re-ruled because a fix touched code
underneath it. **No line moves into or out of slice 5's `Closes:` list.** The
final full-scope pass before ship proves all 166 on the finished product,
which is where the change is accounted for.

Order:

1. **Slice 5 merges with both clauses, and passes verification.**
2. **Then the owner connects the real Body Smart.** F44 is the [OPERATOR] line
   that fires the collision. Its evidence is produced after step 1 and not
   before. Connecting first does not corrupt anything irreversibly, but it
   spends real weigh-ins on days where a fabricated reading may win the column,
   and those days cannot be re-decided later because the table refuses UPDATE.
3. **Slice 13 is unaffected and is not a prerequisite.** This ruling writes no
   row and removes none, which is most of why it was chosen over the
   alternatives. Any option that removes seeded rows must wait for slice 13,
   because slice 13 is where backup and restore first exist and F131 is where a
   tested, audited, category-scoped deletion path first exists. Until then the
   only copy of `a7bf989c` is the running database.
4. **After slice 13, optionally, the fabricated history can go.** F131 already
   requires permanent deletion by category behind a typed confirmation phrase,
   with a zero-row query afterwards, and it already has to solve deleting from
   an append-only table. "Demonstration data" is a category. That is the
   sanctioned path and it costs no new rubric line and no new hatch. It is not
   required by anything: the rows are honestly marked and the screens no longer
   prefer them.

### The check worth running before slice 5 merges

Read-only, as `vernier_suite`, which holds SELECT and nothing else. It answers
whether the collision already exists on this install for waist, and it is the
census the slice 5 verifier should print anyway:

```sql
select metric, date(measured_at at time zone 'Europe/Paris') as day,
       count(*) filter (where not seed) as real,
       count(*) filter (where seed) as seeded
from measurement
where correction_index = 0
group by 1, 2 having count(*) > 1
order by 2 desc, 1;
```

The seed writes waist at 07:20 and the owner's real waist landed at 08:54:34 on
2026-08-23, so if a seeded waist exists for that date the two are already
sharing a day. If they are, the display already prefers the real one by clock
accident, which is exactly the accident this ruling removes.

## 4. What the owner must do himself

**One thing, and only one.**

**Do not connect the real Body Smart until slice 5 has merged and passed
verification.** After that, connect it whenever he likes.

Nothing else. No truncate, no re-seed, no environment change, no psql prompt.
Specifically:

- **Do not turn `DEMO_MODE` off.** It does not do what it sounds like. See
  section 2.
- **Do not truncate before slice 13.** `a7bf989c`, waist 101.5 cm, manual,
  2026-08-23 08:54:34, is the only real measurement this database holds and
  there is no backup in the tree. TRUNCATE is a table-level operation the
  row-level trigger does not see, which is why it works and why it is dangerous.
  If the fabricated history is ever to be removed, it is removed after slice 13
  ships backup, restore and F131, through F131.

## 5. Rubric lines affected

**No line is falsified by a real connection. No amendment is warranted. The
count stands at 166 and nothing in `.forge/DOD.md` is touched.**

Line by line, on the eight read for this ruling:

- **F25**, ticked. "Demonstration data loads only when demonstration mode is on
  and stamps every row with source `demo`; with it off the seed refuses to run
  and the tables stay at zero rows." Unaffected. "Only when" is a necessary
  condition, not a sufficient one, so a seed that additionally yields to a real
  reading still satisfies it. The evidence is both runs on an empty database
  and both row counts, and on an empty database the yield set is empty and the
  counts are identical. If anything the clause is harder to fail afterwards.
- **F28**, ticked. Stroke widths and dash arrays read off the rendered markup.
  Independent of which row fills a column.
- **F29**, ticked. Goal visible on Today, editable from Health, trajectory
  redraws. `goalValueOn` reads the goal, not the readings.
- **F30**, ticked. **Not falsified.** The line is a rule about how absence is
  drawn and it holds over any series. What changes is where it is re-observed:
  the seeded gap at anchor offsets 10 to 13 could be filled by a historical
  import, so the owner's install may stop being the place to photograph it. It
  already is not: `tests/e2e/trend-scratch.spec.ts` runs on the scratch
  instance, which no real Withings row will ever reach. Re-observable without
  touching production, which is the requirement.
- **F31**, ticked. Weight and waist share one axis, a point reaches its source
  in two taps. Unaffected, and improved: after clause 2 the dot the owner taps
  is the measured row rather than possibly the fabricated one.
- **F32**, ticked. Five signals, present and absent, never zero. Withings sends
  none of the five. Its absent evidence was taken on a scratch instance at
  `DEMO_MODE=0` with zero measurements, which nothing here touches.
- **F36**, unticked, slice 5's. Affected, and this ruling names the defect it
  would otherwise hide: in demonstration mode the importer's `demo` rows meet
  the seed's `demo` rows and `decideDedup` stores both as `unique`. The line
  says "every imported row carries source `demo`", which would pass while the
  day census is wrong. **The line is right and the build must meet it.** The
  fix is the same edit as clause 2's sibling in section 2: step 3 stops
  requiring a different source. No amendment.
- **F40**, unticked, slice 5's. Two notes, neither an amendment.

  First, an evidence hazard rather than a line problem: "the same import twice
  produces the same row count" is measured on a table the demonstration
  continuation is also writing to, once per screen read, whenever the calendar
  day advances. Three runs that straddle midnight differ for a reason that has
  nothing to do with the import. The counts must be scoped to the import's own
  rows or to a fixed day census. The builder's day-census test already prints
  the right shape.

  Second, F40's second clause, "a corrected group arriving with a newer
  modification time updates in place and appends a provenance entry rather than
  inserting a second row", against A1's plan text, "inserts with `ON CONFLICT DO
  NOTHING` ... or records a dedup decision when the row already exists". A1
  established that an UPDATE is impossible here, and left the rubric untouched,
  correctly. F40 is still satisfiable and it is not mis-specified: a correction
  row is one reading with a provenance entry behind it, which is what "updates
  in place ... rather than inserting a second row" describes at the level the
  line is written at, the reading and not the tuple. The build has to append a
  correction to the chain rather than a second independent reading. That is
  slice 5's work and its verifier's call. **Not amended, not softened, and not
  pre-ruled here.**

Two further lines named for the record, neither amended and neither re-ruled:

- **F34**, ticked. Not falsified and not re-ruled, per `.forge/PROCESS.md`
  section 1. Worth knowing for whoever produces slice 5's evidence: the seed's
  Withings-shaped source records keep `readFreshness` reporting Withings fresh
  on this install, so the stale chip cannot be observed here for that source
  outside the seeded quiet windows. That is a property of the seed, not of the
  line, and clause 1 removes it as soon as real weight arrives.
- **F43**, unticked, slice 5's. Same cause, and it is the line that will be
  blocked by it. Its evidence, "name the stale Withings data by cause with the

  seed rows are being written, or on the owner's install after clause 1 has
  stopped them. Naming the path now so slice 5's verifier does not lose a round
  to it.

## Disclosure to the owner, in four sentences

Your demonstration data and your real scale would have shared days, and on a
day where both existed the app could have drawn the fabricated reading and
hidden the measured one, because the part that stores a value ranks sources and
the part that draws it only looks at the clock. Both now follow one rule: a
measured reading always wins its day, and the seed stops writing any metric you
have real data for. Nothing is deleted, nothing is overwritten, your one real
row is untouched, and no rubric line moved. **Connect the Body Smart after
slice 5 passes, not before, and do not turn `DEMO_MODE` off: it would not clear
the demonstration data, it would only freeze it on your screen.**
